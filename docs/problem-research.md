# Problem research: is this a real problem?

Desk research on 2026-11-06. No interviews yet. Each finding is labeled by source strength.

## Verdict
The problem is real and widely documented. Commission artists already write terms of service specifically to prevent it. The open question is whether artists want a tool for it, rather than whether the problem exists. That question is not answered yet.

## Evidence

### 1. Artists publish terms of service that target this exact problem (strong, primary, many independent examples)
- A guide on commission ToS lists the four problems a ToS exists to prevent: **scope creep** ("endless 'tiny tweaks' until the entire piece is redrawn"), **ghosting** before the final balance, **chargebacks**, and **surprise usage** such as merchandise. It recommends 2 revision rounds at sketch and 1 at color, extra fees for additions, commercial use at 1.5x base, and a 50% deposit with the rest after sketch approval or before final delivery. ([Don Corgi](https://doncorgi.com/blog/art-commission-terms-of-service/))
- A widely shared sample ToS: one major revision before line art, then minor only; "additional charges for excessive revisions"; commercial use not included unless agreed, at an extra rate such as 2x; deposit or full payment upfront; invoices due within a set time; "client warrants they will not issue chargebacks". ([Artists Aware](https://artistsaware.com/art-commissions/sample-tos-detailed/))
- Individual artists' published terms: revision limits and paid extra revisions ($15 per revision; one free round then $60; five free then 10% of price), commercial rights at a multiple of the price, and refusal of requests "that essentially increase the project scope". ([radstos](https://radstos.carrd.co/), [cjkkjh](https://www.cjkkjh.com/commissions-terms-and-conditions), [tapirclip](https://tapirclip.substack.com/p/tos), [SylviaPaints](https://www.sylviasykesart.com/blog/sample-artist-commission-contract))

What this shows: the structure we model is how artists already work: deposit, sketch approval, final, revision limits, priced extras, separate commercial license. Today it lives in a static document that the client may never read, and enforcement happens by chat.

### 2. Freelancers broadly struggle to get paid (strong, but older and not specific to artists)
- Freelancers Union, 2015, over 5,000 US freelancers: 71% had trouble collecting payment at least once in their career; half had trouble in the prior year; the average unpaid freelancer lost almost $6,000 a year, about 13% of income. ([Freelancers Union](https://blog.freelancersunion.org/2015/12/10/costs-nonpayment/))
- Caveat: US-only, 2015, all freelancers. Use for context, not as an artist statistic.

### 3. Creative freelancers report regular unpaid work (medium, secondary reporting)
- The Accountancy Partnership survey of 1,060 creative freelancers (UK): 45.9% work at least five unpaid hours per week, as reported by [The Stage](https://www.thestage.co.uk/news/nearly-half-of-creative-freelancers-regularly-work-unpaid-hours-report). Other figures (80% regularly working unpaid hours) come from secondary coverage we could not open ([Prolific North](https://www.prolificnorth.co.uk/news/more-than-half-of-creative-freelancers-report-rising-stress-as-budgets-tighten-and-unpaid-work-grows/), blocked). Verify before quoting.
- Unpaid hours are not the same as scope creep. Do not equate them.

### 4. Cross-border commissions are a burden for Korean artists (weak to medium, anecdotal)
- A Korean artist on X recommends VGen "if taking commissions directly from foreigners feels burdensome", because PayPal can be connected through the platform for a fee. ([X post](https://x.com/minisunnyworld/status/1974154450454696217), quoted from search snippet)
- Korean artists warn each other about overseas commission scams that use PayPal and fake documents. ([Threads](https://www.threads.com/@irre_illust/post/DCxy-_PSIwG?hl=ko), [arca.live](https://arca.live/b/3d3d/145453507))
- These show friction and distrust in direct overseas commissions. They do not measure scope creep.

### 5. Do not use
- "Over 80% of freelancers experience scope creep" and "$2,000–$5,000 lost per year" from scope-creep vendor blogs ([stopscopecreep.com](https://stopscopecreep.com/blog/scope-creep-statistics), [cleartimeline.com](https://www.cleartimeline.com/problems/scope-creep)). These are from tool vendors and their original sources could not be verified (one page failed to load).

## Counter-evidence and limits (be honest about these)
- **Existing solutions.** VGen offers commission requests, standard usage licenses, and PayPal payout through the platform ([VGen help](https://help.vgen.co/hc/en-us/sections/12709560612503-Commissions), [licenses](https://help.vgen.co/hc/en-us/articles/29122353993495-Standard-Usage-Licenses)). Artist status is invite-only. Our angle is commissions that arrive by DM or email outside a marketplace, plus change orders checked against the agreed scope.
- **Scope creep may not be the top pain.** The same sources rank ghosting, chargebacks, and scams alongside it. Our product does not prevent chargebacks or scams, and PayPal invoices do not make a service refund-proof.
- **Small tickets.** Many commissions are small. Some artists may not want a tool for a $30 piece; the fit is better for larger, staged work such as full illustrations and VTuber assets.
- **No demand evidence.** No interviews, no usage, no willingness-to-pay data. Say so.

## How to state it in the pitch
> Commission artists already write terms of service to limit revisions, price extras, and separate commercial use, because "just one more change" is a known way to lose money. Those terms sit in a document the client may not read, while the actual agreement happens in a chat, often across languages. CreatorMilestone turns that chat into an agreed scope, checks later requests against it, and bills the approved extras through PayPal.

Cite sources 1 and 2 with their caveats. Treat demand as a hypothesis until artists confirm it.
